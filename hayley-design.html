Agent journey mapping across 4 workflows
The Problem

Independent insurance agents were navigating a fragmented portal cobbled together from multiple disconnected systems. They'd submit an application, get no confirmation, then spend the rest of the day fielding client calls asking "where's my policy?" — with no good answer. Status visibility was invisible. Client data lived everywhere and nowhere.

What We Did
Agent interviews Card sorting Journey mapping Dashboard strategy Systems analysis Stakeholder alignment
The Insight

Before designing anything, we mapped four core agent workflows and identified a single cross-cutting pain point: agents had no way to know what was happening with an application after they submitted it. Everything else — fragmented tools, manual re-entry, support call volume — cascaded from that one gap.

Strategic Direction

Rather than redesigning screens, we reframed the problem around a unified client contact system and a prioritized activity feed — giving agents immediate visibility into what needs attention the moment they log in. The design direction connected directly to reducing inbound support calls and improving agent retention, giving leadership a business case, not just a design pitch.

The Problem

Stockguard built a web app to modernize livestock insurance — eliminating the back-and-forth email chains needed to get a quote. The functionality worked. The experience didn't. The interface was bare-bones, navigation was unclear, and there was no coherent design system holding it together.

What We Did
UX audit Competitor analysis Wireframing High-fidelity redesign Design system Usability review
The Approach

This was a self-initiated redesign — no client brief, no stakeholder direction. Just a broken product and a better way forward. Audited the existing app, identified hierarchy and navigation failures, and redesigned with a scalable component system and clear user flows.

The Outcome

Before-and-after visuals tell the story clearly: a product that once felt unfinished now communicates trust and ease. The redesign demonstrates how strategic design thinking — not just visual polish — transforms how a product performs in the hands of real users.

Lots Live — Real-Time Parking App
⊙

Home — real-time lot availability
⊞

Reservation — streamlined booking
The Problem

Students and faculty were arriving at campus parking lots only to find them full — wasting time, spiking stress, and causing missed classes. The uncertainty of not knowing was often worse than the problem itself. Existing parking apps didn't solve for real-time visibility or advance reservations in a campus context.

What We Did
User surveys Interviews Card sorting Personas Wireframing Usability testing High-fidelity prototype
The Insight

Research revealed that users didn't just want to find parking — they wanted to stop thinking about parking altogether. The design response was a minimal, real-time interface that surfaced availability and reservations front-and-center, removing every unnecessary step from the flow.

The Outcome

Usability testing showed reduced task completion time and significantly lower reported stress. Iterative refinement — adding back buttons, tightening visual consistency, smoothing screen transitions — turned a functional prototype into an experience users actually trusted.
